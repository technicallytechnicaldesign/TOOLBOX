Anatomy of a Feature Control Frame

Every GD&T callout lives in a boxed frame with a fixed left-to-right order. Learn the compartments once and every frame you meet afterwards reads the same way.

A leader runs from the frame to the feature it controls — a surface, an axis, a hole. The frame itself is read strictly left to right: what's controlled, how big the zone is, and (for anything but a pure form tolerance) which datums the zone is measured from, in the order that matters most first.

  1. Characteristic symbol — which geometric property is controlled. Position, here.
  2. Diameter symbol (⌀) — present only when the zone is cylindrical, not a pair of parallel planes.
  3. Tolerance value — the width, or diameter, of the zone the feature has to fall within.
  4. Material condition modifier — Ⓜ/Ⓛ applied to the tolerance, or nothing at all for RFS, the default.
  5. Primary datum — read left to right in precedence order; this one constrains the most degrees of freedom.
  6. Secondary datum — can carry its own modifier, independent of the tolerance's modifier.
  7. Tertiary datum — the last reference needed to fully define the datum reference frame.

Pure form tolerances — straightness, flatness, circularity, cylindricity — never take a datum reference: a feature's own shape doesn't get measured relative to anything else. See Characteristic Symbols below.

The Geometric Characteristic Symbols

Twelve current symbols, five families. What each one bounds is the whole story — everything else in the frame just says how big and relative to what.

Form — no datum reference

Straightness

A line element, or a feature's derived median line, has to fall within a tolerance zone that's itself a straight line (or a cylinder, for an axis).

Flatness

An entire surface has to fall between two parallel planes separated by the tolerance value — independent of any other feature.

Circularity (Roundness)

Any single cross-section perpendicular to an axis has to fall between two concentric circles — checked independently, plane by plane.

Cylindricity

Circularity's 3-D cousin: the whole surface at once has to fall between two concentric cylinders — roundness, straightness and taper all bounded together.

Profile — can be used with or without a datum

Profile of a Line

A 2-D cross-section of a surface has to fall within a tolerance band straddling the true profile — checked one slice at a time.

Profile of a Surface

The whole 3-D surface has to fall within a tolerance band around the true profile — the general-purpose form-and-location tolerance for anything that isn't flat or round.

Orientation — always relative to a datum

Angularity

A surface or axis has to sit at a specified basic angle to a datum, within a tolerance zone of parallel planes (or a cylinder, for an axis).

Perpendicularity

The 90° case of angularity — a surface or axis has to be square to a datum within the stated zone.

Parallelism

The 0° case — a surface or axis has to stay parallel to a datum within the stated zone.

Location — always relative to a datum

Position

A feature's true (theoretically exact) location is defined by basic dimensions; the actual feature has to fall within a zone — usually cylindrical — centred on it. The most common location tolerance by far.

Legacy drawing alert Concentricity and symmetry are not characteristic symbols in ASME Y14.5-2018. You can still meet them on drawings made to earlier editions. Treat the drawing's named edition as part of the requirement, and do not silently translate a legacy callout into position, profile or runout without engineering review.

Runout — always relative to a datum axis

Circular Runout

As the part rotates a full turn about a datum axis, each individual circular measurement plane can't wobble more than the tolerance, checked independently.

Total Runout

The same rotation, but checked across the entire surface at once — folds in circularity, straightness, taper and coaxiality together, all relative to the datum axis.

Runout tolerances never take a material condition modifier — size and form are inherently mixed into the check, so MMC/LMC don't apply.

Datums & Precedence

A datum is a real feature on the part — a face, a bore, an axis — nominated as an origin. Everything else that references it gets measured from there, not from the part's nominal CAD geometry.

Datum feature symbol

A filled or open triangle sits on the feature itself — a whole face, not a point — with a leader up to a boxed reference letter.

Referenced in a frame

The same letter, dropped into a datum-reference compartment, ties the tolerance zone back to that specific feature.

A single datum rarely pins down every degree of freedom, which is why frames commonly reference two or three, read left to right in order of precedence:

OrderRoleConstrains
1st listedPrimary datumThe most degrees of freedom — typically the feature the part sits flattest against.
2nd listedSecondary datumFewer remaining degrees of freedom, established against the primary.
3rd listedTertiary datumWhatever's left — usually just one rotational degree of freedom.

A datum feature reference can use a maximum or least material boundary modifier, MMB or LMB, independently of the tolerance's MMC/LMC modifier. This permits a movable datum simulator whose effective boundary relates to the part, most often used for a datum that's itself a hole or a pin. A datum target — a specific point, line or small area used in place of an entire feature, common on castings and other unfinished surfaces — has its own symbol family and is worth a direct look at the standard rather than guessing at the figure here.

Modifiers & Related Marks

These don't name a tolerance type on their own — they change what a tolerance value or a datum reference actually means.

MarkNameMeaning
ⓂMMC — Maximum Material ConditionThe tolerance applies at MMC; as the feature departs from MMC, it earns bonus tolerance.
ⓁLMC — Least Material ConditionThe tolerance applies at LMC — used to hold a minimum wall thickness or edge distance.
—RFS — Regardless of Feature SizeThe default when no modifier is shown at all: the tolerance doesn't change with the feature's actual size.
ⓅProjected Tolerance ZoneThe zone projects above the surface into space rather than through the feature — common on threaded and press-fit holes, where the mating part's position matters more than the hole's own axis.
ⓉTangent PlaneThe feature is constrained by a plane tangent to its high points, not by its full, wavy derived surface.
ⒻFree StateThe tolerance applies with the part unrestrained by any external force — for parts flexible enough that clamping would hide a real deviation.
STStatistical ToleranceThe tolerance is assigned by statistical methods rather than worst-case arithmetic; the parts have to be produced under statistical process control.

Diameter (⌀)

The tolerance zone (or the dimension it's attached to) is a diameter, not a linear width or a pair of flat planes.

Basic Dimension

A boxed value: an exact, theoretical dimension with no tolerance of its own — the tolerance lives entirely in the feature control frame instead.

Worked Examples

Full frames, decoded left to right.

A — Position, Three Datums

  1. Characteristic: position — a hole's location.
  2. Zone: ⌀0.010, cylindrical.
  3. Modifier: MMC on the tolerance — bonus tolerance grows as the hole gets bigger.
  4. Datums: A primary, datum feature B secondary at MMB, C tertiary.

B — Flatness, No Datum

  1. Characteristic: flatness — a surface's own shape.
  2. Zone: 0.005 between two parallel planes — no ⌀, since the zone isn't a diameter.
  3. Datums: none. Form tolerances control a feature relative to itself, so no reference frame applies.

C — Perpendicularity to One Datum

  1. Characteristic: perpendicularity — a surface square to a datum.
  2. Zone: 0.002 between two parallel planes.
  3. Datum: A — the surface has to be square to datum A within that zone.

D — Profile of a Surface, Two Datums

  1. Characteristic: profile of a surface.
  2. Zone: 0.010, straddling the true profile unless a note says otherwise.
  3. Datums: A primary, B secondary — they orient and locate the profile zone in space.

E — Circular Runout

  1. Characteristic: circular runout.
  2. Zone: 0.004, checked at each measurement plane as the part rotates.
  3. Datum: A — the axis of rotation.
  4. No modifier possible: runout never takes MMC/LMC, on this or any frame.
